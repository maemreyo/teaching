## Vocabulary Learning Plan: The Purpose of Knowledge (3 Videos)

This is a detailed plan to learn all vocabulary words from the reading passage, divided into 3 manageable parts. Each part corresponds to a video/learning session and explores the ethical dimensions of knowledge and its application.

### VIDEO 1: The 'Arson for Profit' Dilemma (10 words)

**Video Script Prompt**

*   **Target a specific use case:** "Introduce the central ethical dilemma of the passage using the provocative example of an 'Arson for Profit' course at a university."
*   **Focus on a specific source:** "Focus on the first 10 vocabulary words. For each, provide a short English meaning and a simple example sentence from the text."
    1.  found
    2.  institution
    3.  motto
    4.  arson
    5.  profit
    6.  prospective
    7.  prosecution
    8.  criticize
    9.  occupations
    10. illegal
*   **Describe the show structure:**
    *   "Start with the university's ideal: 'I would **found** an **institution** where any person can find instruction in any subject. That was the founders' **motto** for Cornell University.'"
    *   "Next, present the provocative example: 'But what would they have thought of a course called **Arson** for **Profit**? It's a real course. It's intended for **prospective** arson investigators, teaching them how to establish evidence for effective **prosecution**. My point is not to **criticize** such programs, which are part of the professionalization of many **occupations**. However, it suggests how dishonest and **illegal** behavior can creep into any aspect of life.'"
    *   "Then, create a review screen for the 10 words."
    *   "End with a question: 'What other university courses can you think of where the knowledge could be used for both good and **illegal** purposes?'"

**Video Script Prompt**

*   **Focus on a specific source:** "Focus on the next 10 vocabulary words. For each, provide a short English meaning and a simple example sentence from the text."

    13. cue
    14. principles
    15. codified
    16. assumed
    17. obvious
    18. downright
    19. signed up
    20. irrelevant
*   **Describe the show structure:**
    *   "Start with the marketing class scenario: 'I was invited to speak to a marketing class. The instructor **appreciates** the **ethical** perspective I bring. I took my **cue** from the course title: **Principles** of Marketing. I asked the students, "Is marketing principled?" A subject can be **codified** with rules without being ethical.'"
    *   "Next, discuss the students' reaction: 'Many students immediately **assumed** the answer was **obvious**: no. I then made a suggestion that may sound **downright** crazy: that marketing is by definition principled. The students have **signed up** to learn how to market, but to what end?     *   "Then, create a review screen for the 10 words."
    *   "End with a question: 'Can a field like marketing have **principles** without being **ethical**? What's the difference?'"

### VIDEO 3: The Means and the End (10 words)

**Video Script Prompt**

*   **Target a specific use case:** "Conclude by applying Kant's philosophical idea that a field is defined by both its means and its end, arguing that the misuse of knowledge negates the profession itself."
*   **Focus on a specific source:** "Focus on the final 10 vocabulary words. For each, provide a short English meaning and a simple example sentence from the text."
    21. expertise
    22. proposal
    23. endeavor
    24. scrutiny
    25. achieve
    26. supremely
    27. presumably
    28. prosecute
    29. articulate
    30. generalize to
    31. reckless
    32. fraud
    33. identical
*   **Describe the show structure:**
    *   "Start with the two attitudes: 'One attitude is that the purpose of marketing is to sell things. The other is that the purpose is irrelevant to the acquisition of marketing **expertise**. My **proposal** is that neither is correct. A professional **endeavor** is defined by both its means and its end, and both deserve **scrutiny**. We need to study how to **achieve** a goal, and also what that goal is.'"
    *   "Next, apply this to the arson course: 'This is where 'Arson for Profit' becomes **supremely** relevant. That course is **presumably** about means: how to detect and **prosecute** crime. When I ask students to **articulate** the purpose of their field, they **generalize to** the safety and welfare of society. Using that same knowledge for **reckless**, destructive activity is not firefighting; it's arson. Using marketing principles in an unprincipled way is not marketing; it is **fraud**. A doctor and a poisoner can use the **identical** knowledge for opposite ends.'"
    *   "Then, create a review screen for the 10 words."
    *   "End with a question: 'What is the **supremely** important difference between a professional **endeavor** and simple **expertise**?'"
