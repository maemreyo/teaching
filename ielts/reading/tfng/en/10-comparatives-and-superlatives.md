# Comparatives and Superlatives

Of course. Comparative structures are one of the most common and effective "traps" to test the golden rule of reading: **"Only rely on the information provided in the text."**

Below is a detailed analysis table of Comparative and Superlative Structures, focusing on how they are used to create traps.

### Table of Comparative & Superlative Structures

| Criteria | Comparatives | Superlatives |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | Compares **two objects or two groups of objects** based on a certain attribute (more, less, equal). | Compares **one object with all other objects** in the same group to determine the highest or lowest position. |
| **Common structures** | - `adjective/adverb-er than...`<br>- `more/less... than...`<br>- `as... as...`<br>- `not as... as...`<br>- `different from...`<br>- `similar to...` | - `the adjective/adverb-est`<br>- `the most/least...`<br>- `the first/last/best/worst...`<br>- `one of the...` |
| **Example in a sentence** | *"Company A is **larger than** company B."* | *"The Amazon is **the largest** rainforest in the world."* |
| **Requirement for 'True'** | The reading passage must **directly compare** the two objects. | The reading passage must **clearly state** that the object is at the top or bottom of the ranking in the mentioned group. |

-----

### Result and Application to the T/F/NG Test

**Golden Rule:** For a question containing a comparison to be **True** or **False**, that comparison **must explicitly exist** in the reading passage. Otherwise, the answer is always **Not Given**.

Let's consider 3 practical situations:

#### Situation 1: The Most Classic Trap → NOT GIVEN

This is the most common case. The reading passage provides information about objects but does not compare them.

  * **Result:** If the reading passage only describes objects individually, any question comparing them will be **Not Given**.
  * **Example:**
      * **Question:** *The Amazon rainforest has **the highest** number of plant species in the world.* 
      * **Text:** *The Amazon rainforest contains thousands of plant species. The Congo Basin in Africa is also home to a rich and diverse range of flora.*
      * ➡️ **Analysis:** The passage mentions two forests (Amazon and Congo) and states that both are diverse. However, it **does not compare** the number of species between them, nor **does it claim** that the Amazon is the world's number one.
      * ➡️ **Answer: Not Given**.

#### Situation 2: Direct Contradiction → FALSE

The reading passage also makes a comparison, but the result is completely opposite to the question.

  * **Result:** When the reading passage presents a comparison that contradicts the question, the answer is **False**.
  * **Example:**
      * **Question:** *In the UK, railways are **more popular than** buses.* 
      * **Text:** *While millions of people use the UK's rail network each year, statistics show that the number of passenger journeys made by bus is significantly **higher than** those made by train.*
      * ➡️ **Analysis:** The question states railways > buses. The text asserts buses > railways. This is a direct contradiction.
      * ➡️ **Answer: False**.

#### Situation 3: Information Match → TRUE

This is the least common case. The reading passage provides exact information to confirm the comparison in the question.

  * **Result:** When the reading passage performs the exact comparison in the question (even if using different wording), the answer is **True**.
  * **Example:**
      * **Question:** *The literacy rate for women was **lower than** the rate for men in 1950.* 
      * **Text:** *In the mid-20th century, statistics show that the rate of literacy for men stood at 70%, whereas the corresponding figure for women was approximately 50%.*
      * ➡️ **Analysis:** The passage provides figures (50% < 70%) that allow us to accurately make the "lower than" comparison in the question.
      * ➡️ **Answer: True**.
