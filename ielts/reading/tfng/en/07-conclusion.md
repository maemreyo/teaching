# Part VII: Conclusion

This report has conducted a deep decoding of the ambiguity of the True/False/Not Given question type in the IELTS Reading test, approaching it from an interdisciplinary perspective of Linguistics and Cognitive Psychology. Through detailed analysis, a central thesis has been formed and demonstrated: the ambiguity of this question type is not a random characteristic or a flaw, but a deliberately and sophisticatedly designed construct, aimed at assessing higher-order cognitive skills that go beyond conventional reading comprehension.

## 7.1. Summary of the Main Thesis

The core thesis of this report is that the ambiguity in the T/F/NG question type is systematically created through the combination of two main factors:

1.  **Precise Linguistic Tools (Semantic Distractors):** Test designers use a range of linguistic tools such as quantifiers, comparative structures, adverbs of degree, and sophisticated paraphrasing techniques. These elements are not intended to trick arbitrarily, but to create "near misses" in meaning, forcing candidates to shift from keyword matching to a deep and precise analysis of the entire meaning of the sentence. They are micro-tests of attention to detail and the ability to recognize linguistic nuances.
2.  **Exploitation of Inherent Cognitive Loopholes:** These linguistic elements achieve maximum effectiveness because they target the weaknesses and natural tendencies in the human information processing system. Specifically, this question type:
    - **Creates an "Inference Trap":** By forbidding the use of background knowledge, the test forces candidates to inhibit a natural and efficient cognitive process of "gap-filling inference," creating a conflict between everyday reading habits and the artificial rules of the test.
    - **Induces Cognitive Overload:** Time pressure, the complexity of the text, and the presence of distractors together create an enormous cognitive load, reducing the capacity of working memory and leading to errors due to omission.
    - **Activates Confirmation Bias:** This high-pressure and ambiguous environment is fertile ground for confirmation bias, causing candidates to tend to search for evidence to support their initial judgment rather than assessing it objectively. The struggle between 'False' and 'Not Given' is essentially a test of the ability to overcome this bias and recognize the absence of evidence.

In summary, the T/F/NG question type does not just ask "Do you understand the text?" but also poses deeper questions: "Can you apply a rigorous logical system?", "Can you recognize the limits of the evidence provided?", and "Can you control your automatic thinking tendencies under pressure?".

## 7.2. A Paradigm Shift

From the analysis above, a directional conclusion for teaching and learning this question type is that a paradigm shift is needed. Instead of approaching T/F/NG as a collection of discrete tips and tactics, we need to see it as a reasoning skill that needs to be built systematically.

Success lies not in memorizing rules, but in developing a **meta-cognitive, evidence-based process**. The 5-step Evidence-Based Reasoning (E-BR) process proposed in this report is a model for that shift. It is not just a method for doing the test, but a way of training the mind:

- It forces the learner to **slow down**, analyzing the question purposefully before diving into the search.
- It requires the learner to **establish logical standards** for each answer, creating an objective evaluation framework.
- It guides a **forensic evidence mapping**, focusing on absolute precision.
- And most importantly, it integrates **validation steps**, a conscious defense mechanism against cognitive biases.

By training with such a process, candidates are no longer victims of ambiguity. Instead, they become active analysts, capable of decoding the structure of the test, managing their cognitive resources, and making decisions based on solid logic. The ultimate goal is not just to read faster, but to think like a forensic logician—a skill that not only helps conquer IELTS but also has profound value in academic and professional environments.
