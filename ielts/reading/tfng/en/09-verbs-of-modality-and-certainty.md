# Verbs of Modality and Certainty

| Criteria | Verbs of Modality / Uncertainty | Verbs of Certainty / Fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | Presents information as an opinion, hypothesis, suggestion, or an unverified possibility. This is the language of discussion and research. | Presents information as a proven fact, a confirmed result, or an indisputable reality. |
| **Common words** | - suggest, indicate, point to<br>- claim, argue, state<br>- believe, think, assume<br>- seem, appear<br>- may, might, could, can<br>- be likely to, be possible | - prove, demonstrate, show<br>- confirm, establish, determine<br>- know, recognize<br>- ensure, guarantee<br>- it is a fact that...<br>- will, must |
| **Example in a sentence** | `The research suggests that a new teaching method may be effective.` | `The research proves that this new teaching method is effective.` |
| **Implication** | The writer does not commit 100% to the truthfulness of the information. There is still room for doubt or other results. | The writer asserts the truthfulness of the information with certainty. The information is presented as a verified fact. |

## Result and Application to the T/F/NG Test

The most important result you need to remember is: The trap lies in the shift in the level of certainty between the reading passage and the question.

This is the most classic trap situation:

    Text: Uses a verb of Modality/Uncertainty.

        Example: "...many people believed it was the Eighth Wonder of the World..."

    Question: Uses a verb of Certainty/Fact.

        Example: It is known that the tunnel was the Eighth Wonder of the World.

➡️ Analysis: The passage only says this was a "belief" of many people, i.e., an opinion, not a fact. The question, however, asserts it as a "known fact". This upgrade from an uncertain opinion to a certain fact creates a direct contradiction.

➡️ Answer: False

Mastering this distinction table will help you quickly spot when a question is deliberately "exaggerating" or "overstating" the level of certainty compared to what is written in the reading passage.
