# Quantifiers and Absolutes

Certainly. The group of quantifiers and absolute words are the most powerful and easiest to detect distractors. They act like "black or white" logical switches, making the determination of the answer often very decisive.

Here is a detailed analysis table.

### Analysis Table of Quantifiers & Absolutes

| Criteria | Absolutes | Non-absolute Quantifiers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | Makes a statement that covers **100% or 0%** of a group. They leave no room for any exceptions. | Describes a **part, a portion, or an unspecified quantity** of a group. They always imply that there are exceptions. |
| **Common words** | **100%:** `all, every, each, always, only, solely, entirely, completely`<br><br>**0%:** `none, no, never, nobody, nothing` | `most, many, the majority of`<br>`some, several, a number of`<br>`a few, few, a minority of`<br>`often, sometimes` (can also be classified here) |
| **Requirement for 'True'** | The reading passage must state with certainty that there are **no exceptions**. | The reading passage only needs to confirm that it is true for **at least a portion** of the group. |

-----

### Result and Application to the T/F/NG Test

**Golden Rule:** The most classic trap lies in the contradiction between an **absolute (100% or 0%)** statement in the question and a **limited (partial)** statement in the reading passage.

#### Situation 1: The Most Common Trap → FALSE

This is the scenario you will encounter most often. The question uses an absolute word, but the reading passage only supports a narrower scope.

  * **Result:** When the question says "All" but the reading passage only confirms for "Some/Few/Many," the answer is **False**.
  * **Example:**
      * **Question:** ***All** students find reading difficult.* 
      * **Text:** ***Some** students find reading difficult. However, **many** excel at it.*
      * ➡️ **Analysis:** The word "All" (100%) in the question directly contradicts "Some" and "many" (partial) in the text. The presence of "many excelling" further confirms that not "all" find it difficult.
      * ➡️ **Answer: False**.

#### Situation 2: Reverse Trap (0%) → FALSE

Similar to above, but with absolute negative words.

  * **Result:** When the question says "No" but the reading passage indicates that **at least one case** exists, the answer is **False**.
  * **Example:**
      * **Question:** ***No** solutions to the problem have been found.* 
      * **Text:** *While a definitive cure remains elusive, **a few** potential solutions have been proposed by the research team.*
      * ➡️ **Analysis:** "No" (0%) directly contradicts "a few" (meaning > 0).
      * ➡️ **Answer: False**.

#### Situation 3: Absolute Confirmation → TRUE

This case is rarer, as it requires the reading passage to use equally strong language to confirm the absolute statement.

  * **Result:** When the reading passage uses different wording but confirms the 100% or 0% scope of the question, the answer is **True**.
  * **Example:**
      * **Question:** ***Every** participant in the study was over the age of 18.* 
      * **Text:** *The study's entry criteria required **all** applicants to be adults, and the final cohort included individuals aged 19 to 45, with **no exceptions**.*
      * ➡️ **Analysis:** "Every" in the question is confirmed by "all" and "no exceptions" in the text.
      * ➡️ **Answer: True**.
