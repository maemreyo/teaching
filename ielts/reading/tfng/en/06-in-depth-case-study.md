# Part VI: In-depth Case Study: Applying the Process

Theories and analytical frameworks are only truly useful when they are applied in practice. This section will illustrate the power of the 5-Step Evidence-Based Reasoning (E-BR) process by applying it in detail to a set of real T/F/NG questions from IELTS preparation materials. This process will make abstract concepts like "semantic distractors" and "confirmation bias" concrete, showing how the E-BR process works as a navigational tool to arrive at the correct answer logically and confidently.

## 6.1. Choice of Reading Passage and Questions

For analysis, we will use a commonly used reading passage and question set, the one about the **The Thames Tunnel**. This passage and its related questions have appeared in the guidance materials of reputable IELTS experts and contain typical examples for all three answer types: True, False, and Not Given.⁵

**Passage (Relevant Excerpt):**
The Thames Tunnel was a tunnel built under the River Thames in London, England. It was the first subaqueous tunnel ever built and many believed it was the Eighth Wonder of the World at the time it was opened. The tunnel was built between 1825 and 1843 using new tunnelling shield technology... It was a triumph of civil engineering, but a commercial failure. The aim had been for the tunnel to be used by wheeled vehicles to transport cargo so that it could bring in a profit. However, it was never used for this purpose. Instead, it became a tourist attraction. In its first three months, it attracted one million people, which is half of London's population at the time. It was said that people came from far and wide to see the world's first underwater tunnel. In 1865, the tunnel became part of the London Underground system which continues to be its use today.

**Questions:**

1.  The Thames Tunnel was the first tunnel ever built under a river.
2.  The Thames Tunnel was considered by many people to be the Eighth Wonder of the World.
3.  The tunnel was used more by the middle and upper classes.
4.  The aim of the tunnel was to turn a profit as a tourist attraction.

## 6.2. Applying the E-BR Process to Each Question

We will go through each question, strictly applying the 5-step process.

### Question 1: The Thames Tunnel was the first tunnel ever built under a river.

- **Step 1: Statement Deconstruction**
  - **Core Proposition:** The Thames Tunnel was the first tunnel.
  - **Semantic Distractors:** "the first... ever built" (superlative/uniqueness) and "under a river" (specific location).
- **Step 2: Hypothesis Formulation**
  - **True:** The text must explicitly state that this was the first tunnel built under a river.
  - **False:** The text must state that another tunnel was built under a river before it, or that this was not the first.
  - **Not Given:** The text describes the tunnel but does not mention its construction order compared to other tunnels.
- **Step 3: Evidence Search**
  - Scan the text with the keywords "Thames Tunnel", "first", "tunnel", "under a river".
  - Locate the sentence: "It was the first subaqueous tunnel ever built...".¹⁸
- **Step 4: Evidence Mapping**
  - **Question:** "the first tunnel ever built under a river".
  - **Text:** "the first subaqueous tunnel ever built".
  - **Analysis:** The key is to understand the word "subaqueous". "Sub" means "under," and "aqua" relates to "water."¹⁸ Therefore, "subaqueous" means "underwater." "Under a river" is a specific case of "underwater" (subaqueous). The text confirms it was "the first". All components of the question are directly confirmed by the text.
- **Step 5: Conclusion Validation**
  - **Decision:** The information matches completely. The answer is **True**.
  - **Bias Check:** There is no inference here. The connection is made directly through vocabulary knowledge.

### Question 2: The Thames Tunnel was considered by many people to be the Eighth Wonder of the World.

- **Step 1: Statement Deconstruction**
  - **Core Proposition:** The tunnel was considered the Eighth Wonder of the World.
  - **Semantic Distractor:** "by many people" (quantifier).
- **Step 2: Hypothesis Formulation**
  - **True:** The text must state that "many people" or an equivalent phrase believed it was the Eighth Wonder.
  - **False:** The text must state that few or no one believed it, or that it was considered something else.
  - **Not Given:** The text might mention the Eighth Wonder but not who or how many people held that view.
- **Step 3: Evidence Search**
  - Scan the text with the keywords "Eighth Wonder of the World".
  - Locate the sentence: "...many believed it was the Eighth Wonder of the World at the time it was opened.".¹⁸
- **Step 4: Evidence Mapping**
  - **Question:** "considered by many people".
  - **Text:** "many believed".
  - **Analysis:** "Considered" and "believed" are synonyms in this context. "By many people" matches perfectly with "many" in the text. All components match.
- **Step 5: Conclusion Validation**
  - **Decision:** The information matches completely. The answer is **True**.
  - **Bias Check:** Some candidates might be distracted by a similar but different question: "The Thames Tunnel was the Eighth Wonder of the World." That would be 'False' because the text only states it was a belief, not a recognized fact.¹⁸ However, the current question includes the element "considered by many people," which makes it match the text.

### Question 3: The tunnel was used more by the middle and upper classes.

- **Step 1: Statement Deconstruction**
  - **Core Proposition:** The tunnel was used by social classes.
  - **Semantic Distractor:** "more by the middle and upper classes" (comparative structure).
- **Step 2: Hypothesis Formulation**
  - **True:** The text must compare the usage levels and state that the middle and upper classes used it more than other classes.
  - **False:** The text must compare and state that the working class used it more, or that all classes used it equally.
  - **Not Given:** The text may talk about tourists or users of the tunnel but not compare the usage levels between different social classes.
- **Step 3: Evidence Search**
  - Scan the text for information about the tunnel's users.
  - Locate the sentences: "it became a tourist attraction. In its first three months, it attracted one million people... It was said that people came from far and wide...".
  - **Search Analysis:** The text mentions "people" and "tourists" in a general way. There are no keywords related to "class", "middle class", "upper class", or "working class".
- **Step 4: Evidence Mapping**
  - **Question:** Requires a specific comparison of usage between social classes.
  - **Text:** Provides absolutely no information about the social classes of the users.
  - **Analysis:** There is no information in the text to confirm or deny the comparative claim.
- **Step 5: Conclusion Validation**
  - **Decision:** Because there is no evidence, the answer is **Not Given**.
  - **Bias Check:** A candidate might fall into the trap of gap-filling inference, assuming that tourist attractions in the 19th century were mainly for the upper classes. This is using background knowledge, which is forbidden by the rules of the test. The E-BR process forces us to conclude based on the absence of evidence in the text.

### Question 4: The aim of the tunnel was to turn a profit as a tourist attraction.

- **Step 1: Statement Deconstruction**
  - **Core Proposition:** The aim of the tunnel was to make a profit.
  - **Semantic Distractor:** "as a tourist attraction" (specifying the purpose).
- **Step 2: Hypothesis Formulation**
  - **True:** The text must state that the original purpose was to build it as a profitable tourist attraction.
  - **False:** The text must state that the original purpose was something else.
  - **Not Given:** The text does not mention the original purpose of the tunnel.
- **Step 3: Evidence Search**
  - Scan the text with the keywords "aim", "profit", "tourist attraction".
  - Locate the sentence: "The aim had been for the tunnel to be used by wheeled vehicles to transport cargo so that it could bring in a profit.".¹⁸
- **Step 4: Evidence Mapping**
  - **Question:** The aim was to profit as a tourist attraction.
  - **Text:** The aim was to profit by transporting cargo.
  - **Analysis:** Both refer to the aim of making a profit. However, the means to achieve that purpose are completely different. The question says "tourist attraction," while the text says "transport cargo." This is a direct contradiction regarding the nature of the original plan. The text also adds that it _became_ a tourist attraction, implying this was not the original aim.
- **Step 5: Conclusion Validation**
  - **Decision:** The information in the question directly contradicts the evidence in the text. The answer is **False**.
  - **Bias Check:** A candidate might be confused because both "profit" and "tourist attraction" appear in the text. Confirmation bias might cause them to focus only on these keywords and ignore the logical relationship between them. The E-BR process, with its detailed mapping step, clarifies that although the words are present, the overall meaning is contradictory.
