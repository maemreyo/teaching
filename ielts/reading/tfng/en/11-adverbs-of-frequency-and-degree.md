# Adverbs of Frequency and Degree

Alright. We will continue with the group of adverbs of frequency and degree. This is one of the most subtle types of distractors, as they do not create a clear contradiction like absolute words (all/none) but rather a "gray area," requiring careful judgment from the candidate.

### Analysis Table of Adverbs of Frequency & Degree

| Criteria | Adverbs of Frequency | Adverbs of Degree |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | Describes **how often** an action or event occurs. (How often?) | Describes the **extent, scope, or importance** of an action or quality. (To what extent? / How much?) |
| **Common words** | - **100%:** `always`<br>- **~80-90%:** `usually, normally, generally`<br>- **~60-70%:** `often, frequently`<br>- **~20-40%:** `sometimes, occasionally`<br>- **~5-10%:** `rarely, seldom, hardly ever`<br>- **0%:** `never` | - **Mainly:** `mainly, primarily, largely, mostly`<br>- **Completely:** `completely, entirely, fully`<br>- **Significantly:** `significantly, considerably`<br>- **Partially:** `partly, partially`<br>- **Slightly:** `slightly` |
| **Requirement for 'True'** | The reading passage must provide information that allows you to confirm that frequency. | The reading passage must provide information that allows you to confirm that scope or emphasis. |

-----

### Result and Application to the T/F/NG Test

**Golden Rule:** The trap of this group of words lies in the fact that the question often introduces an adverb (e.g., *quickly*, *mainly*) to "embellish" the information. Your task is to check whether that "embellished" detail actually exists in the reading passage.

#### Situation 1: The Most Classic Trap → NOT GIVEN

This scenario accounts for the majority of trap questions using adverbs. The reading passage confirms that the main event occurred, but **does not mention** its frequency or degree.

  * **Result:** If the question adds an adverb of frequency/degree that the reading passage does not mention, the answer is **Not Given**.
  * **Example:**
      * **Question:** *The uptake of second languages in Australian schools has developed **quickly**.* 
      * **Text:** *In recent years, there has been a significant growth in the number of students enrolling in second language courses across Australian schools.*
      * ➡️ **Analysis:** Both the reading passage and the question agree that second language learning has "developed/grown." However, the reading passage provides no information about the **speed** (quick or slow) of that development. The word "quickly" in the question is new, unconfirmable information.
      * ➡️ **Answer: Not Given**.

#### Situation 2: Direct Contradiction → FALSE

This case occurs when the reading passage also uses an adverb of frequency/degree, but it carries a meaning opposite to the adverb in the question.

  * **Result:** When the reading passage presents a frequency/degree that contradicts the question, the answer is **False**.
  * **Example:**
      * **Question:** *The project was **entirely** successful.* 
      * **Text:** *While the project achieved some of its key objectives, it was only **partially** successful due to significant budget overruns.*
      * ➡️ **Analysis:** "Entirely" (100%) directly contradicts "partially" (a part).
      * ➡️ **Answer: False**.

#### Situation 3: Information Match → TRUE

This case is less common, where the reading passage uses different wording but confirms the exact frequency/degree stated in the question.

  * **Result:** When the reading passage confirms the degree stated in the question, the answer is **True**.
  * **Example:**
      * **Question:** *Storms **frequently** occur in this region during winter.* 
      * **Text:** *In the winter months, it is not uncommon for this region to experience storms several times a month.*
      * ➡️ **Analysis:** "Several times a month" is a reasonable interpretation of "frequently."
      * ➡️ **Answer: True**.
