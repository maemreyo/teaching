Paradigm Shift: Time không chỉ là "numbers to write"

→ Time expressions là structural indicators của conversation flow

🕐 Clock Time Formats

12-Hour Format (Standard)

Digital style: 9:30 AM, 2:15 PM, 11:45 PM

Spoken style: Half past nine, Quarter past two, Quarter to twelve

Formal style: Nine thirty in the morning, Two fifteen in the afternoon

24-Hour Format (Military/International)

Digital: 14:30, 09:15, 23:45

Spoken: Fourteen thirty, Zero nine fifteen, Twenty-three forty-five

Context: Official schedules, international settings

Natural Speech Patterns

Approximate: Around 3, About half past seven

Ranges: Between 9 and 10, From 2 to 4

Relative: In 15 minutes, Half an hour later

📊 Time Expression Analysis

Critical Distinction Patterns:

Exact vs Approximate

Exact: "The class starts at 9:15 sharp"

Approximate: "We usually begin around 9:15"

Point vs Duration

Point: "The meeting is at 2 PM"

Duration: "The meeting lasts for 2 hours"

⚠️ Common Format Traps

❌ Trap Examples:

  • • "3:30" vs "3:13" (number reversal)
  • • "Quarter past" vs "Quarter to"
  • • AM/PM confusion in conversation

✅ Prevention Strategy:

  • • Listen for context clues
  • • Wait for confirmation
  • • Note speaker corrections

Duration Expressions Mastery

Key Concept: Duration expressions indicate "how long" something takes, not "when" it happens. This distinction is crucial for accurate answers.

⏱️ Standard Duration Formats

Hours & Minutes

2 hours 30 minutes • 1 hour 45 minutes • 3 and a half hours

Minutes Only

90 minutes • Ninety minutes • An hour and a half

Approximate Durations

About 2 hours • Roughly 90 minutes • Up to 3 hours

📅 Extended Time Periods

Days & Weeks

3 days • Two weeks • A fortnight • A month-long course

Academic Terms

One semester • 12-week course • Full academic year

🔄 Context Applications

Class Duration Context

"How long is each lesson?"
→ Expected: Duration expression

Answers: "90 minutes" / "1 hour 30 minutes" / "An hour and a half"

Service Time Context

"How long does the treatment take?"
→ Expected: Duration expression

Answers: "45 minutes" / "Three quarters of an hour"

⚡ Speed Recognition Tips

Question Word Analysis:

"How long..." → Duration expected

"What time..." → Specific time expected

"When..." → Time point expected

"Until when..." → End time expected

Time Conversion Strategies

🔄 Essential Conversion Skills

Why conversions matter: Speakers often express the same time in different formats. Students must recognize equivalent expressions to avoid distractor traps.

📐 Quick Conversion Reference

Minutes ↔ Hours

90 min = 1.5 hours = 1h 30m

120 min = 2 hours

45 min = 3/4 hour = 0.75h

30 min = 1/2 hour = 0.5h

15 min = 1/4 hour = 0.25h

12h ↔ 24h Format

2:30 PM = 14:30

9:15 AM = 09:15

11:45 PM = 23:45

12:30 AM = 00:30

12:30 PM = 12:30

Spoken ↔ Digital

Half past two = 2:30

Quarter to four = 3:45

Ten past nine = 9:10

Twenty to eight = 7:40

Five to twelve = 11:55

🧮 Mental Math for Time

Time Addition

Example: Class starts 9:45, runs 1h 30m

Calculation: 9:45 + 1:30 = 11:15

Mental steps:

  • • 9:45 + 1:00 = 10:45
  • • 10:45 + 0:30 = 11:15

Time Subtraction

Example: Movie ends 11:30, runs 2h 15m

Calculation: 11:30 - 2:15 = 9:15

Mental steps:

  • • 11:30 - 2:00 = 9:30
  • • 9:30 - 0:15 = 9:15

🎯 Practice Application

Real Conversation Example:

Speaker A: "The workshop runs for ninety minutes."
Speaker B: "So that's an hour and a half, starting at 2:15..."
Speaker A: "That's right, finishing at quarter to four."

Time expressions identified:

  • • "ninety minutes" = "hour and a half" = 90 min = 1h 30m
  • • "2:15" = start time
  • • "quarter to four" = 3:45 = end time
  • • Verification: 2:15 + 1:30 = 3:45 ✓

Strategic Time Recognition

🎯 Pre-listening Preparation

Form Scanning for Time Clues:

  • • Look for time-related blanks
  • • Identify format expectations (12h/24h)
  • • Note duration vs point-in-time questions
  • • Predict time range contexts

Example Analysis:

"Class time: _____ to _____"

→ Expect: Time range
→ Format: Likely 12-hour
→ Context: Academic schedule

👂 Active Listening Strategies

Signal Word Recognition

Duration Signals:

  • • "for" (for 2 hours)
  • • "lasts" (lasts 90 minutes)
  • • "takes" (takes 45 minutes)
  • • "runs" (runs until 5 PM)

Start/End Signals:

  • • "starts at" (starts at 9)
  • • "begins" (begins 2:30)
  • • "finishes" (finishes 4:15)
  • • "ends" (ends at 6)

Range Signals:

  • • "from...to" (from 9 to 5)
  • • "between" (between 2-4)
  • • "until" (until 3:30)
  • • "through" (through Friday)

Correction Pattern Awareness

Listen for self-corrections and clarifications:

  • • "I mean..." / "Actually..." / "Sorry, that's..."
  • • "No wait, it's..." / "Let me check that..."
  • • "To be precise..." / "More exactly..."
